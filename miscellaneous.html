Algorithmic Elliott Wave Structural Modeling (Glenn Neely Method)

Financial markets alternate between Trends (Impulses) that push prices forward and Pullbacks (Corrections) where prices pause or retrace. Based on Glenn Neely’s Mastering Elliott Wave, this project replaces subjective chart-guessing with an objective mathematical algorithm that turns noisy minute-by-minute price ticks into clear market structure.

🚀 Live Streamlit App GitHub Code

1. Filters Out Noise

Automatically absorbs minor sideways wiggles (<45° angle) and simplifies raw price ticks into clean Major Swings (m1, m2...).

2. Trend vs. Pullback

Distinguishes strong Trend Impulses (:5) from temporary Counter-Trend Pullbacks (:F3, :c3) so you know which direction dominates.

3. Spots Exhaustion

Flags Pattern Endings (:L5 & :L3) when a 5-wave trend, 3-wave pullback, or triangle has completed and a reversal is likely.

4. Objective Math

Uses exact Fibonacci ratios (38.2%, 61.8%, 100%, 161.8%) between adjacent swings instead of subjective human eye-balling.

Stages 1–3: Cleaning & Filtering Raw Prices

  • Step 1 — Raw Monowaves (p. 3-2): Every time price changes direction, it forms a basic building block (M1, M2, ...).
  • Step 2 — Rule of Proportion (pp. 3-3 to 3-8): Checks whether pullbacks are shallow (<61.8%, Directional Trend) or deep (>61.8%, Sideways Consolidation).
  • Step 3 — Rule of Neutrality (pp. 3-9 to 3-13): If a price turn is flatter than 45° (moving sideways rather than up/down), the algorithm absorbs it as noise instead of counting a false wave.

Stages 4–5: Grouping Swings & Naming Patterns

  • Step 4 — Rules of Observation (pp. 3-14 to 3-21): Small internal waves are grouped together until price breaks above the prior swing high or below the prior swing low, producing clean Major Swings (m1, m2, ...).
  • Step 5 — Fibonacci Rules & Structure Labels (pp. 3-22 to 3-67): Measures how much each swing is retraced by the next (|m2/m1|) to classify it as a Trend Impulse (:5, :s5), Pullback (:F3, :c3), or Pattern End (:L5, :L3).
